\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Many civil infrastructure systems are goal-oriented process systems: a resource enters at
one end, passes through a sequence of connected stages, and is delivered at the other. Where the direction of flow is fixed and there is no feedback in normal operation, such a
system can be modelled as a directed acyclic graph. Three of the questions an engineer asks
of it, with what probability supply reaches a point, how much the system can deliver, and
when a dependent sequence completes, each have a method of their own. The methods were developed
separately, each is applied to its own model of the system, and each consumes a single
value for every input, although the failure probabilities, capacities and durations of real
systems are rarely known that well.

This thesis develops the Information Propagation Framework (IPF), in which one directed
acyclic graph model of a system supports reliability, capacity and schedule analysis, the
uncertain inputs are propagated in the form they are stated in wherever the mathematics of
the analysis permits it, and the analyses are provided as a Julia package together with a
no-code interface that runs entirely on the user's machine. The framework rests on a graph
object that carries the topology, a layered processing order and ancestry closures, and on
a decomposition module that identifies every reconvergent subgraph of the network once.

Three toolkits are built on that object. The Probability Propagation Toolkit computes the
exact probability that each node operates and is reached from a source, by conditioning on
the reconvergent structure; applied to interval inputs it returns the exact range, and to
probability-box inputs it returns sound bounds on the distribution of every belief. Its
exactness is validated against a decision-diagram oracle on a corpus of 129 graphs and on
named and adversarial families, and it reproduces the published exact values of two
benchmark networks. The Capacity Flow Toolkit answers six questions about deliverable
throughput, from the binding cut to the most damaging failures and the thresholds at which
service degrades, exact for point-valued capacities and validated against an external flow
library and brute-force enumeration on twelve networks and on generated instances of a
hundred thousand edges. The Critical Path Toolkit generalises the critical path method to a family of operator
pairs and computes exact interval floats by a domination split that reduces the
enumeration to the durations that can bypass each node. It is validated against a path
oracle and exhaustive enumeration, and applied to a published project-scheduling
benchmark. The
framework is registered in the Julia General registry, and its interface is applied end to
end to \CaseStudyNetwork, a real water distribution network, in the closing case study.

The thesis shows that a single network model can serve three analyses without loss to any
of them, that exact reliability with interval and probability-box inputs is practical on
networks of moderate reconvergence, that the cost of exactness is set by the same structural
width that governs the established exact methods, and that these analyses can be delivered
to an engineer who does not program without the system model leaving the engineer's machine.
