\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Many civil infrastructure systems are process systems: a resource enters at one end,
passes through connected stages, and leaves at the other. Where the direction of flow is
fixed, it is a directed acyclic graph. An engineer asks three questions of it --- with
what probability supply reaches a point, how much it can deliver, and when a dependent
sequence completes --- and each has its own method, its own model, and an assumption of
exact inputs that real failure probabilities, capacities and durations rarely meet.

This thesis develops the Information Propagation Framework: one directed acyclic graph
model supports reliability, capacity and schedule analysis; uncertain inputs are
carried in the form they are stated in wherever the mathematics permits; and the analyses
are delivered as a registered Julia package with a no-code interface that runs on the
user's own machine. The three toolkits share one decomposition that identifies every
reconvergent subgraph once.

The probability toolkit computes exact reachability reliability, returning the exact range
for interval inputs and sound distributional bounds for probability boxes, validated
against a decision-diagram oracle and two published benchmarks. The flow toolkit answers
six questions about deliverable throughput, exact for point-valued capacities and
validated against an external solver and exhaustive enumeration. The critical-path toolkit
generalises the critical path method to a family of operator pairs and computes exact
interval floats, validated against a path oracle and a scheduling benchmark. The framework
is then applied end to end to \CaseStudyNetwork, a real water distribution network.

The thesis shows that one model can serve the three analyses without loss, that exact
reliability under interval and probability-box inputs is practical at moderate
reconvergence, that exactness costs the same structural width as the established exact
methods, and that the analyses reach a non-programming engineer without the system model
leaving that engineer's machine.
