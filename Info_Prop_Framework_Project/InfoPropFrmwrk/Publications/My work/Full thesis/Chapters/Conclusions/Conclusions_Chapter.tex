\section{What Was Set Out and What Was Done}
\label{sec:concl-summary}

Chapter~\ref{ch:main-intro} began with a water treatment works and the observation that a
goal-oriented process system, once its direction of flow is fixed, is a directed acyclic
graph. It asked five questions. Can one such model serve the three analyses an engineer runs on
the system? Can the reconvergence that makes a system robust be identified once and
reused? Can exact reliability be computed for interval and probability-box inputs, and at
what cost? Which schedule quantities survive interval durations exactly? And can all of
this reach an engineer who does not program, without the system model leaving the
engineer's machine? This chapter answers each question from the work of the
preceding ones, restates what the thesis contributes, collects the limitations that the
chapters stated individually, and sets out the work that follows from them.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:concl-answers}

\subsection{One model, three analyses}

Chapter~\ref{ch:system-model} defined the network model as a finite directed acyclic graph
with sources, sinks, forks and joins, and built from it a graph object that carries the
topology, a layered processing order whose existence Theorem~\ref{thm:model-layers} guarantees, and the
self-inclusive ancestor and descendant closure of every node. Every analysis in the thesis
reads that object as its first argument, and Chapter~\ref{ch:julia-package} showed that the
code enforces the claim: the three toolkits have no imports between them and share only the
graph object. The three interpretations of the values attached to the model, probabilistic,
capacity, and schedule and cost, were each given a toolkit that answers its question exactly
on the same topology, and Chapter~\ref{ch:integrated-case-study} ran all three on
\CaseStudyNetwork{} from one upload. The answer to the first question is yes, on the model's
four assumptions: independent component behaviour, binary component states under the
probabilistic interpretation, a static topology over the analysis horizon, and acyclicity as
a property of the operating snapshot.

\subsection{Reconvergence identified once}

Chapter~\ref{ch:diamond-module} defined the diamond, a fork whose paths reconverge at a
downstream join, as the default pattern of a generic decomposition module, and proved the
identification complete, deterministic and terminating. Each unique diamond is stored once,
as a graph object of the same form as the network, under an identity that includes the
conditioning context, so that a diamond posed under two different contexts is kept apart
and a diamond posed twice under one context is stored once. The Probability Propagation
Toolkit consumes the module, and Chapter~\ref{ch:front-end} showed a diamond being isolated
and promoted to a network of its own through the interface. The Critical Path Toolkit
computes its own bypass sets and does not use the module; the two structures index
reconvergence at different granularities and neither contains the other. The answer to the
second question is yes for the probabilistic interpretation, and the module is available to
any further analysis that a user adds.

\subsection{Exact reliability under imprecision, and its cost}

Chapter~\ref{ch:probability-toolkit} computed the belief of every node, the probability that
it operates and is reached from a source, by conditioning on the diamonds and recombining by
total probability, and proved the recursion exact. Against a sifted decision-diagram oracle
the beliefs agree to $1.1 \times 10^{-16}$ on 129 graphs and on named and adversarial
families, and the toolkit reproduces the published exact values of a sixteen-node grid at
every sink and of a 23-node power distribution network at one of three regimes, with a
residual of order $10^{-3}$ at the other two that is not a computation error and is left
open. For interval inputs the toolkit returns the exact range, because belief is monotone in
every input, at lower one-shot cost than the decision-diagram route. For probability-box
inputs it returns sound bounds on the distribution of every belief, tight for
high-reliability or weakly reconvergent networks and conservative for deeply reconvergent
networks with broadly uncertain components, at a cost that grows faster than quadratically
with the discretisation level.

The cost question has a plain answer. The toolkit is exponential in the largest conditioning
set the network forces, which is the same structural width that governs a well-ordered
decision diagram, and across the corpus neither method dominates; on independent structure
the two tie and on dense correlated structure the diagram is smaller. The toolkit's case
rests on the two capabilities the diagram does not offer, native interval and
probability-box propagation.

\subsection{Schedule quantities under interval durations}

Chapter~\ref{ch:critical-path} generalised the critical path method to a family of modes,
each an operator pair with the backward semantics its algebra supports, and provided four.
Under interval durations the forward quantities are monotone and their exact bounds follow
from two scalar runs at the corners of the input box. The floats are not monotone, and the
general problem is NP-hard, but the domination split proved that the exact float of a node
is attained with the incomparable durations at one bound, the dominated durations at the
other, and only the bypass durations enumerated, so that the cost is $2\sum_v 2^{|H_v|}$
against $2^k$. On the PSPLIB benchmark instance the split needed 50,524 propagations against
$2^{30}$, and it found three necessarily critical activities that the conservative enclosure
does not identify and removed seven from its possibly critical set. On a dense mesh the
bypass sets approach the whole network and the toolkit falls back to exhaustive
enumeration, as the hardness of the problem requires. The answer to the fourth question is that every forward quantity is computed exactly at
the cost of two runs, and the floats are computed exactly at a cost set by reconvergent
width, with a boundary that the toolkit locates and refuses beyond.

\subsection{Delivery without code and without leaving the machine}

Chapter~\ref{ch:julia-package} described the package that realises every method, registered
in the Julia General registry at version 0.2.1 and verified end to end from a clean install,
and the loopback-only server through which the interface reaches it.
Chapter~\ref{ch:front-end} described the interface: an engineer uploads the files the
package reads, byte for byte, and runs the three toolkits by pressing a button, and the
interface enforces that a value keeps its form, that a result set is what the toolkit
returned, that every result carries its method, and that one analysis runs at a time.
Client and server share one machine and no request leaves it.
Chapter~\ref{ch:integrated-case-study} demonstrated the whole sequence on
\CaseStudyNetwork. The answer to the fifth question is yes, with the qualification that the
interface has been demonstrated and not yet studied in use.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:concl-contributions}

The six contributions listed in Chapter~\ref{ch:main-intro} have each been delivered.
Chapter~\ref{ch:system-model} gave the network model and its graph object.
Chapter~\ref{ch:diamond-module} gave the decomposition module with the diamond as its
default pattern, proved complete, deterministic and terminating.
Chapter~\ref{ch:probability-toolkit} gave the Probability Propagation Toolkit, exact for
point inputs, exact in range for intervals and sound for probability boxes, validated
against decision diagrams, path enumeration and Monte Carlo and against two published
benchmarks. Chapter~\ref{ch:capacity-toolkit} gave the Capacity Flow Toolkit, exact for
point-valued capacities and validated against an external library and brute-force
enumeration. Chapter~\ref{ch:critical-path} gave the Critical Path Toolkit, generalised
over its operator pair and exact for interval floats by the domination split.
Chapters~\ref{ch:julia-package} and~\ref{ch:front-end} delivered all of it as a registered
package and a local no-code interface.

Beyond the list, the thesis contributes a way of working that its chapters practise. Every
result reported is produced by the toolkit and checked against a computation that shares
none of its machinery; every timing is a second-call measurement under a stated
convention; every network carries its provenance and every assigned input is named as
assigned; and the six errors that the validation scripts found, each at a boundary the
synthetic corpus did not reach and a real or benchmark instance did, are recorded with
their fixes. The registered package, the application repository and the validation
repository together let a reader reproduce any chapter's numbers from the cited versions.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:concl-limitations}

The model excludes common-cause failure, degraded component states, reconfiguration during
the analysis horizon, and feedback; a cyclic network is analysed only after orientation.
These are the assumptions of Chapter~\ref{ch:system-model}, and every result is conditional
on them.

The Probability Propagation Toolkit offers no asymptotic advantage over a well-ordered
decision diagram for exact point-valued reliability, and it is practical to about fifty
nodes of moderate reconvergence, with dense or deeply reconvergent structure the limiting
case that every exact method shares. Its probability-box form is practical only for small
networks. Its tighter recombination operator is validated sound on every configuration
tested but not proved, and the power-network reproduction carries an unexplained residual
at two of three regimes.

The Capacity Flow Toolkit is point-valued; a best-case and worst-case pair of capacities is
two solves, not an interval result. Its exactness is on the evaluated candidates where
pruning applies, it computes no probability, and its whole-graph connectivity measures are
zero on any DAG and are left as defined.

The Critical Path Toolkit's domination split is proved and implemented for the LongestPath
mode only and requires point-valued edge values, and the toolkit has no probability-box
form. Whether a proposed new mode has a backward pass is a mathematical question the
implementation does not check.

The package is single-threaded in propagation, performs no conversion from domain formats,
and has a test suite that covers small instances and not the scale or the real networks the
thesis reports. The interface exposes no control over the probability-box discretisation
level and gives no warning before a long run, and it has not been used independently by an
engineer who does not program.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:concl-future}

The work below follows from the limitations, and each item is stated with the chapter that
motivates it.

\begin{itemize}
  \item A soundness proof for the positive-dependence recombination operator, or a tight
    operator whose soundness follows from a theorem (Chapter~\ref{ch:probability-toolkit}).
  \item A depth-limited hybrid for the probability toolkit, conditioning exactly to a stated
    depth and bounding beyond it; an interval outer bound beyond the depth is sound as it
    stands, and the probability-box form would need the Fr\'{e}chet combination there
    (Chapter~\ref{ch:probability-toolkit}).
  \item The residual between the toolkit's exact values and the published values of the
    power distribution network at two regimes, which is not a computation error and whose
    source has not been located (Chapter~\ref{ch:probability-toolkit}).
  \item The shortest-path dual of the domination split, the reduction of interval edge
    values to interval durations by edge subdivision, and the sharing of corner
    evaluations across overlapping bypass sets (Chapter~\ref{ch:critical-path}).
  \item A probability-box form for the critical path toolkit and interval and
    probability-box forms for the flow toolkit, the latter direct for the flow value by
    monotonicity and open for the cut structure and thresholds
    (Chapters~\ref{ch:capacity-toolkit} and~\ref{ch:critical-path}).
  \item A source-to-sink connectivity measure for DAGs, minimum-cost flow, and a
    time-expanded flow model (Chapter~\ref{ch:capacity-toolkit}).
  \item Cyclic networks, by unrolling or by strongly connected components
    (Chapter~\ref{ch:system-model}).
  \item Parallel propagation over the iteration sets with a lock-free diamond store, and a
    test suite that runs the validation corpus on every change
    (Chapter~\ref{ch:julia-package}).
  \item A control and a default for the probability-box discretisation level in the
    interface, with an estimate before a long run, and a study of the interface in use by
    engineers who do not program (Chapter~\ref{ch:front-end}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Closing Remark}
\label{sec:concl-closing}

The framework began from a practical observation about infrastructure data: the topology
of a system is entered three times for three analyses and kept consistent by hand, and the
uncertainty in every input is collapsed to a number before any of them runs. It ends with
one model, three exact analyses that carry that uncertainty through, and an interface that
puts them in the hands of the engineer who holds the data. The case study of
Chapter~\ref{ch:integrated-case-study} \PH{one sentence on what the Net3 run showed, once
the chapter exists}. What the thesis has not done is stated as plainly as what it has, and
the boundaries it maps, of width, of tightness and of scale, are part of its result.
