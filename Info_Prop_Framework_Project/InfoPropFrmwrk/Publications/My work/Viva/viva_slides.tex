\begin{frame}{The problem}
\begin{columns}[T]
\column{0.55\textwidth}
An engineer asks three questions of one system, such as a water network:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Will supply reach each node? \hfill\emph{reliability}
  \item How much can it deliver? \hfill\emph{capacity}
  \item How long to restore it? \hfill\emph{schedule}
\end{itemize}
\medskip
Today each is a separate model, so the \textbf{topology is entered three times} and kept
consistent by hand.

\medskip
Every input is uncertain, but it is \textbf{collapsed to a number} before any analysis runs.
\column{0.42\textwidth}
\textbf{Why it is hard}\\[2pt]
Redundancy is \emph{reconvergence}: routes split and rejoin, sharing components upstream.
The independence assumption of simple propagation then fails, and always
\bad{overstates} reliability.

\medskip
No reviewed tool combines all three analyses on one network, and none propagates intervals
or p-boxes natively.
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{One model, three interpretations}
\begin{columns}[c]
\column{0.42\textwidth}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth,height=0.72\textheight,keepaspectratio]{fig01_framework_overview.pdf}
\column{0.56\textwidth}
\small
A system is a \textbf{DAG}. One \textbf{graph object} (layers, ancestor and descendant
closures, node roles) is read by three toolkits.

\medskip
\textbf{Research questions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Can one DAG model serve all three analyses, topology entered once?
  \item Can reconvergence be identified once and reused?
  \item Exact reliability under intervals and p-boxes: what is guaranteed, at what cost?
  \item Which critical-path quantities stay exact under interval durations?
  \item Can it reach an engineer who does not program, with the model kept on their machine?
\end{enumerate}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ 3
\begin{frame}{Reconvergence, identified once and conditioned on}
\begin{columns}[c]
\column{0.32\textwidth}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth,height=0.55\textheight,keepaspectratio]{fig01_simple_diamond.pdf}\\
\footnotesize every component $0.9$
\column{0.64\textwidth}
\small
Independent update at join 5: \bad{$b(5) = 0.749$}.\quad Exact: \good{$b(5) = 0.675$}.

\medskip
Condition on the fork's reachability, then recombine by total probability:
\[
b(5) = 0.9\,\bigl[\,0.81 \times \underbrace{\bigl(1-(1-0.729)^2\bigr)}_{\text{given fork reached}}
+ 0.19 \times 0\,\bigr] = 0.675462
\]
Two sub-problems replace $2^{10}$ component states.

\medskip
\textbf{Network Decomposition Module}: finds every \emph{diamond} (a fork whose paths reconverge
at a join), stores each unique diamond once with its \textbf{conditioning set}, and is proved
complete, deterministic and terminating.
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ 4
\begin{frame}{Probability Propagation Toolkit: exact, and what it costs}
\begin{columns}[c]
\column{0.5\textwidth}
\small
One pass over the layers, conditioning at each join.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Point inputs}: exact belief; agrees with a sifted BDD to
  $1.1\times10^{-16}$ on 129 graphs and named families.
  \item \textbf{Intervals}: exact range, because belief is monotone in every input.
  \item \textbf{p-boxes}: sound bounds on the distribution of each belief.
\end{itemize}
\medskip
\textbf{Cost}: exponential in the largest conditioning set, the same width that governs a
well-ordered decision diagram. Neither method dominates; the case rests on native interval
and p-box propagation.
\column{0.48\textwidth}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth,height=0.62\textheight,keepaspectratio]{fig08_hero_cdf.pdf}\\
\footnotesize p-box band brackets the Monte Carlo distribution
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

%% ------------------------------------------------------------------ 5
\begin{frame}{Capacity and schedule on the same graph object}
\begin{columns}[T]
\column{0.49\textwidth}
\textbf{Capacity Flow Toolkit} \small (point-valued)
\begin{itemize}
  \item Throughput, minimum cuts, saturated edges, single points of failure, degradation and
  upgrade thresholds, redundancy.
  \item Every minimum cut from one solve: the closed subsets of the free zone
  (Picard and Queyranne), at most $2^{|\mathcal{F}|}$.
  \item Two designs, same $F^\star = 37$: one has 4 minimum cuts, the other 1. Throughput alone
  does not tell them apart.
\end{itemize}
\column{0.49\textwidth}
\textbf{Critical Path Toolkit}
\begin{itemize}
  \small
  \item CPM generalised to a family of operator pairs.
  \item Interval durations: forward quantities exact from two corner runs.
  \item Floats are NP-hard in general. The \textbf{domination split} enumerates only the bypass
  set $H_v$: cost $2\sum_v 2^{|H_v|}$ against $2^k$.
  \item PSPLIB: 50,524 propagations against $2^{30}$; three necessarily critical activities the
  enclosure misses.
\end{itemize}
\end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{End to end: EPANET Net3}
\begin{columns}[T]
\column{0.5\textwidth}
\small
97 nodes, 119 edges, oriented by its own hydraulic simulation; converted once, three toolkits.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Reliability}: worst-served junction $0.81$; $[0.35, 0.99]$ under $\pm5\%$.
  \item \textbf{Capacity}: whole demand met, \good{680.1 L/s}; river pump out,
  \good{43.3 L/s} (6.4\%).
  \item \textbf{Schedule}: 74 days along 28 activities; $[59, 89]$ days under intervals.
\end{itemize}
\column{0.47\textwidth}
\small
The \textbf{river pump} (95--97, \bad{red}) is in every degraded minimum cut \emph{and} on the
restoration critical chain, a link only the two analyses together show.

\begin{frame}{Answers, contributions and limits}
\begin{columns}[T]
\column{0.52\textwidth}
\small
\textbf{Answers}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Yes, on the model's four assumptions.
  \item Yes, for the probabilistic interpretation.
  \item Exact for points and intervals, sound for p-boxes; cost set by conditioning width.
  \item Forward quantities in two runs; floats exact at a cost set by bypass width.
  \item Yes: registered Julia package and a local no-code interface.
\end{enumerate}
\column{0.45\textwidth}
\small
\textbf{Limits, stated in the thesis}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Practical to about fifty nodes of moderate reconvergence; p-boxes on small networks.
  \item Flow toolkit is point-valued.
  \item Acyclicity is a property of one operating snapshot.
  \item The interface is demonstrated, not yet studied in use.
\end{itemize}
\medskip
\textbf{Package}: \texttt{InformationPropagationAnalysis.jl}\\
\textbf{Demo}: \texttt{information-propagation-framework.dev}
\end{columns}

\begin{frame}{Backup: why the degraded flow is so low}
\small
\begin{itemize}
  \item With the river pump out, the sources are the lake pump and one discharging tank.
  \item In the snapshot orientation they reach only six junctions (7, 8 and 77--81), which are
  fully served; the other 52 receive less than their demand.
  \item The derated trunk main does not bind: its only feed is the river pump.
  \item Physically, the lake pump could push water through pipes that ran the other way at the
  snapshot. The oriented model cannot represent that. A degraded state needs an orientation of
  its own.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}
